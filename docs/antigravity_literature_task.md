# Prompt for Antigravity: literature review only

Please work on the literature-review track for our university project, **AI-Based Cardiovascular Risk Assessment System Using Explainable Machine Learning**. Do not modify training code, datasets, model results, or the prediction target. Our exact research target is **10-year cardiovascular mortality** (death from heart disease or cerebrovascular disease), among adults aged 40-79 with no self-reported prior cardiovascular disease at baseline. We use public US NHANES 1999-2008 baseline data linked to CDC 2019 public-use mortality. The current eight predictors are age, NHANES recorded male/female category, systolic blood pressure, BMI, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, current smoking, and self-reported diabetes. This is a research prototype, not a clinical tool.

Create `docs/literature_review_matrix_v1.md` with **20-30 verifiable, strong sources**, prioritizing original peer-reviewed studies, official risk-model documentation, and reporting standards. For every source, give full citation, DOI or stable link, population/country, sample size, outcome and time horizon, predictors, model family, validation method, AUROC/AUPRC if reported, calibration measures if reported, and explainability method if reported. Write `not reported` rather than guessing. Separate **fatal CVD mortality** studies from broader incident/nonfatal CVD studies so their performance is not compared as if outcomes matched.

Include a concise synthesis (about 1-2 pages) of: logistic regression versus random forest/boosted trees for CVD risk; why calibration matters; missing-data handling; external validation and transport from US data to another population; the limits of SHAP as a non-causal model explanation; and TRIPOD+AI reporting. Identify 3-5 concrete gaps our study can address and cite the sources for each claim. Verify that every DOI/URL resolves and that numbers are copied accurately from the source. Do not invent papers or results. If you cannot access full text, mark what you verified from an abstract only. End with a list of unresolved questions for the team.

Use only `docs/literature_review_matrix_v1.md` for the deliverable, so it can be reviewed and merged into the research protocol later. No patient data, personal records, or private hospital sources are needed.
